Notes on slow cooking

By Priya Shah · 3 days ago · 6 min read

For most of my twenties I believed that good food needed constant attention. A stew was something you stood over, stirring and tasting, until the kitchen smelled right. It took a long winter and a broken oven before I tried the opposite approach.

The idea is simple. You brown the meat properly, add the aromatics and a little wine, then leave the pot on the lowest heat for most of a day. The collagen in the shoulder slowly turns to silk, and the sauce thickens without any help from flour.

What surprised me most was how much time I got back. I could read a chapter of a novel, walk the dog, or simply sit by the window while the house filled with the smell of thyme and bay. The stew did not need me, and that turned out to be a relief.

There are mistakes to avoid. Do not let the liquid boil hard, because the meat will tighten and turn stringy. Salt in stages, tasting near the end, since the sauce reduces as it rests. And finish with something bright, a squeeze of lemon or a spoon of vinegar, to lift the richness.

Patience is the only real ingredient in a good braise.

Next time I want to try a white bean version with fennel and smoked paprika. I will report back, though I suspect the results will be just as forgiving as the beef.
