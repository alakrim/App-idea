Harbour storm leaves thousands without power

By Maya Lindqvist

Published 11 October 2026, 08:14 BST · 19 hours ago

Share this on Facebook Share on X Email

The storm that moved up the coast overnight brought winds of more than ninety kilometres an hour to the harbour district, and by breakfast time the regional grid operator had confirmed that about forty thousand homes were without electricity.

Crews worked through the night to clear fallen trees from the main road into the port. Residents of the old fishing quarter said the sea wall held, but spray reached the second floor of several houses on Mill Street, and many families spent the night in the community hall.

Ruth Okonkwo, who runs a bakery near the quay, said she had lost a whole morning of trade but was grateful that the ovens had been running on gas. She opened a little after seven with candles on the counter and handed out bread to neighbours who had come to check on their homes.

Forecasters expect the wind to ease by the afternoon, although a second band of rain is due overnight. The emergency services asked people to stay away from the seafront until the council has inspected the promenade for damage to railings and lamp posts.

Power companies said they hoped to restore supply to most customers by Sunday evening. Engineers are working on substations that were flooded during the storm, and the operator warned that some rural properties could wait longer because cables there must be replaced rather than repaired.

The regional council will meet on Monday to decide whether to open a relief fund for small businesses. The deputy mayor said the damage to the harbour would take months to repair, and she urged residents to keep a record of any losses for insurance claims.
